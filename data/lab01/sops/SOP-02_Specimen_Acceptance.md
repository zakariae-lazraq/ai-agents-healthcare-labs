# SOP-02 — Specimen acceptance and rejection

*Northbridge Clinical & Research Laboratory — FICTIONAL document written for training. Not for clinical use.*

*Version 3.0 · Effective 2026-01-15 · Owner: Specimen Reception · Approved: Laboratory Director*

## 1. Purpose

This procedure defines when a specimen received by the laboratory is accepted or rejected, and how a rejection is documented and communicated.

## 2. Labelling

Every primary tube must carry two patient identifiers — full name and date of birth, or full name and medical record number (MRN, format NB- followed by 6 digits) — and the collection date and time. Unlabelled specimens are rejected. Mislabelled specimens are rejected and are never relabelled by the laboratory.

## 3. Haemolysis

The analyser measures a haemolysis index (H-index) on every serum and plasma sample. Potassium is not reported when the H-index is above 100: the sample is rejected for potassium and a new sample is requested. 25-hydroxyvitamin D is not affected by haemolysis up to an H-index of 500.

## 4. Volume

Minimum sample volumes are those of the laboratory test catalogue; for 25-hydroxyvitamin D, 0.5 mL of serum. Specimens below the minimum volume are rejected.

## 5. Stability and storage

25-hydroxyvitamin D in serum is stable for 7 days at room temperature (15–25 °C), 14 days at 2–8 °C and 3 months at −20 °C. Potassium: unseparated blood is stable for 4 hours at room temperature and must not be refrigerated; separated plasma is stable for 7 days at 2–8 °C. Specimens received outside these limits are rejected.

## 6. Documentation of rejection

Every rejection is recorded in the laboratory information system with the reason, and the requesting unit is notified within 1 hour.
